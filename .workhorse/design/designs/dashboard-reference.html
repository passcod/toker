Why each thing is coloured

Header. The  ?  and ⇄ chips are reversed, the only handles on the page. no data is red because the ledger has no row at all, which is the same reading as a proxy that has gone quiet — written out rather than shown as a zero age.

Sessions. 1M is green and bold for a native million-token ceiling, the best case; 200k is yellow for a known ceiling that is not that; the dim ? is an unknown one. The $ marks a session released past the armed gate, and the ↑ one served on a newer model than it asked for, bright because it was the latest turn. Each - is dim: not reported, which is not zero. The idle column dims only past three minutes, so 10m is dim and 1m is not.

Context. Occupancy bars are cyan, which is identity rather than judgement — a share is not good or bad. ses-crow sits at 85%, over the threshold that would turn it red and add ← compacts soon, but it has been quiet ten minutes, so the bar dims instead and the row says idle 10m. A quiet session is not about to compact.

Tokens. The ▬ proportion bars are dim throughout: they annotate the counts, which are the row's point. At 94.3% the hit rate falls in the yellow band, and only the bar carries that colour — the figure itself stays plain, because plain is the most prominent thing on the screen. The note and the 2 req unknown caveat are dim.

Rate. Empty minutes are a dim · rather than a gap, so a quiet stretch reads as quiet. No bucket here holds an error; one that did would be bold red. The sparkline spans the whole window at any width, folding minutes into wider buckets rather than dropping the oldest.

What is not coloured is most of it. Names, counts, models, column headings and panel borders are all plain, and plain white sits at 14:1 against the ground while red sits at 3.77:1. The screen reads as a calm readout because colour is doing less work than an author expects.
