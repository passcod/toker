Direction D, the things that are not tables

Providers, settings and revisions are not ordered rules, so they do not live in the chain. They are the config page, whose strip is a plain row of tabs with no arrows. Providers are the interfaces the chain ends at, and say what routes to them.

Providers, one broken — 120×40

The operator pressed Tab twice from the dashboard. anthropic_sub is on, not configured, credential none, and is selected. Where the current page states this and offers nothing, the panel says what it means, what to do about it ([e] enter a key, [space] turn it off), that the other credential sources are chosen by toker setup, and what is affected: five rewrites, two defaults and a route send requests to it. Each provider row carries the same used-by count, so the interface list is wired to the chain it ends.

Settings, a field refusing a value — 120×40

The operator pressed 2 and e on cold min tokens and typed 175k. The refusal is beside the field, in red, and saves are blocked until it is fixed: the row cannot be committed and the control says why. Settings keep the grouped label-and-value rows that already read well, with a line of what each does, and the two read-only rows sit under their own header and are dim.

History: revisions as sentences — 120×40

The operator pressed 3. Each save above became a revision, so history reads as a log of what changed. A revision is described by a sentence derived from its diff, and the panel for the selected one says what moved and which requests it affected; no JSON, and nothing to review before it applies. [x] makes an old revision live again, as a new revision.
