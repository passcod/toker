RATE & QUOTA across accounts

The quota block fans its accounts out sideways, sharing the one label column. A machine with a single account renders exactly as it does today, and each further account costs no rows at all beyond the one naming them — which matters, because the block is never shed.

A meter row's bar is one width across the accounts in it, so the seven-day bars can be read against each other and the five-hour bars against each other. Rows differ freely, because a five-hour bar and a seven-day one are not comparable in the first place. The overage row has dropped its red rejected prefix to stay above the bar's ten-cell floor — the renderer's existing order, unchanged.

Below the width two addresses need, accounts show by number. The strip scrolls sideways to reach the rest, so it needs no key of its own: the wheel and the arrows that already move between views carry it, and nothing new enters the legend. … N more is the list-cut phrase the dashboard already uses, turned on its side — a column at the edge rather than a line at the foot.

The strip comes to rest on whole accounts. Scrolling by the cell would leave a bar cut down the middle, and a cut bar reads as a smaller share — the one comparison the columns exist to make, read wrong. The marker moves to whichever side is hiding something, so the cut is never silent.
