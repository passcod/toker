The idle column always carries a one-cell stage slot and a space, blank on a quiet row. A request in flight fills the slot, and its age, padded to three cells, replaces the idle age, so the table holds still and the marks line up. ‖ held, ⇡ sent, ⇣ receiving: dashed, so they stay apart from the model column's ↑. A lone request's mark is plain, since a stage is not a judgement.

Proposed: parallel requests

The mark stands for the longest-running request, and its style says how many others run beside it: plain for none (ses-new), bold for one more (ses-free), cyan bold for two or more (ses-hot). Cyan marks identity rather than judgement, so it says "busy" without saying "worry". The column is the same width as in the quiet frame. Under the frame, the same three cells as NO_COLOR renders them: both parallel steps keep their bold, so "more than one" survives even though one more and several more then look alike.

Legend rows

‖ ⇡ ⇣   in flight: not yet sent, awaiting a reply, or streaming; timed from arrival
⇣ ⇣   bold: one more request in flight beside it; cyan: two or more

These replace the held sent recv and +N rows in the dashboard's ? overlay, and the matching rows in the design language. The session detail popup keeps the words and its per-request lines: nothing there reflows, and it is where the exact count is read.

Worth a look

The exact count leaves the table. The dashboard spec asks a row to show how many requests are in flight. Colour banding gives one, two, or three or more, so the spec's wording changes with this, and the detail popup carries the exact number.
